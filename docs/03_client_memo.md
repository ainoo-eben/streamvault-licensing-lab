# Memo to Kwame

**From:** Analyst
**Date:** 2026-11-22
**Subject:** $14M licensing decision — franchise bundles vs standalone hits

## Recommendation
Buy **standalone hits**, not franchise bundles. Standalones clear both a commercial bar (profitable) and an audience bar (high rating) more often than bundles do. Bundles look strong on raw revenue, but that is driven by one or two blockbusters carrying several weak middle entries. If you license a bundle, you are paying for films that would not earn their keep on their own.

## Two-window comparison
The answer does not change between windows, but it narrows. Across all years, standalones hit at 96% vs bundles at 72%. From 2000 onward, standalones hit at 73% vs bundles at 68%. The gap shrinks because recent franchises are stronger, but standalones still lead. Pre-2000 figures are reported as-is, with no inflation adjustment.

## Shortlist
**Three franchise targets:** The Godfather, The Dark Knight, The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King — each a genuine hit with a strong audience score, but each bundle contains weaker entries you would also be buying.

**Five standalone targets:** The Shawshank Redemption, Schindler's List, Interstellar, Spirited Away, Pulp Fiction — all clear both bars with no weak middle entries to carry.

## Talent read
Directors and lead actors with at least 3-5 films on the list: Christopher Nolan, Peter Jackson, Steven Spielberg (directors); Daniel Radcliffe, Tom Hanks, Robert Downey Jr. (actors) show the strongest and most consistent records. Each name rests on only 3-8 films, so treat as a signal, not a guarantee.

## Deal note
For the strongest franchise target (The Godfather), pay up to a modest premium over the median standalone. The single number that most drove its ranking is its audience rating — if that rating were lower, it would drop out of the top tier. Any bid above the standalone median needs sign-off.
